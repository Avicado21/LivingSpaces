One layout for an empty room and a full one

Zones, not a slot list

  • Back wall = seating. Couch centred, the mini fridge as its end table, the floor lamp at the other arm, the bookshelf in the corner.
  • Centre = the rug. It sits under the coffee table with the couch's front legs on it, so even two items look like a "living room".
  • Left wall = media. TV centred, the speaker in the back corner, and the console on the TV stand. With no TV yet, the console waits on the floor where the stand would go, then hops onto the stand when a TV arrives.
  • Front right = reading chair, angled at the coffee table. A second chair stacks behind it (×2) instead of crowding the room.
  • Front left stays open as the walkway under the window. That's where the Add marker floats, so nothing ever lands in the way.
  • Every zone works on its own: a lone chair, lamp or rug looks deliberate, not misplaced (see the 1- and 5-item shots below). The rug unrolls in place instead of dropping through furniture.

"Support HEIC … Android … convert to PNG"

Any phone photo becomes a PNG

The real format is read from the file's first bytes, because iPhones often upload HEIC with no file type. JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, GIF and BMP use the browser's decoder. HEIC/HEIF uses it where it can (Safari) and otherwise loads libheif. The photo is then resized (long edge ≤ 1568 px), re-encoded as PNG and kept under 3.75 MB. The card shows each step: received, decoded, converted, classified. Tested with a real HEIC with an empty file type, a JPEG, and a TIFF (refused in Chrome with a clear message). RAW/DNG is refused with a "save it as JPEG or HEIC" prompt.

"Multiple of the same type … an arrow"

One model per type, arrows in its menu

Adding a second couch doesn't add a second couch to the room. The existing one bounces, and a small ×2 chip appears above it. Click it and the menu shows "Couch · 1 of 2" with ‹ › arrows (the arrow keys work too) to flip between each couch's photo, price and who paid. Moving one out keeps the model until the last one leaves. Chad also understands groups: "Tell me about our chairs" lists both.

Where AWS plugs in

Architecture (same for all three)

The mockups never call AWS. Two functions in the shared code mark the seams: mockClassify() for Claude Haiku and mockChatReply() for Claude Sonnet. Each mockup also has a "How it's built" note.

Seam 1 · classification (Claude Haiku)

  • Photo goes to S3 through a presigned URL; /classify sends it as an image block to anthropic.claude-haiku-5-5 through the Bedrock Mantle Messages client.
  • Structured outputs (JSON schema) pin category to an enum of the four supported types, plus confidence and a suggested name. Adding a category means one enum value and one generic model.
  • The UI always lets a roommate override the result; low confidence (<70%) is flagged.

Seam 2 · mediator chat (Claude Sonnet)

  • /chat streams anthropic.claude-sonnet-5-5 over SSE. The system prompt is the mediator persona plus houseContext(): roommates, bills, items, who owes whom.
  • Option C also uses this seam for "Ask Roomie for a fair price" at move-out.
  • Lambda holds the Bedrock IAM role; the browser only ever has a Cognito token.

What differs per option

  • A: nothing extra on the backend.
  • B: items also store x, z, rotation; move-out stores the agreed value.
  • C: one extra prompt for fair-price suggestions; camera shots are client-only.
Recommendation

Build on A, borrow three ideas

Use Option A's fixed isometric diorama and view-offset drawers as the base. It is the closest match to the reference image, it gives the smoothest version of "the room shifts when a tab opens", and it is the cheapest to build and keep consistent across screen sizes.

CPhase lighting from C. Morning for move-in, day for living, golden hour for move-out. It makes the three phases feel distinct without changing screens, and it costs almost nothing on a fixed camera.
CThe in-world add card from C in place of A's modal, so adding an item never covers the room. The step order can stay A's (photo, check type, details).
BThe buyout value step from B. Purchase price, a 20%/yr suggestion, or custom. Without it, whoever keeps a two-year-old couch pays full price. Optionally add B's drag-to-place later.

Why not B as the base

The app-style columns and orbit are flexible, but they move the art direction away from the reference, the bottom sheets cover half the stage during the two key moments (adding and moving out), and resizing the canvas through every frame of a transition is the heaviest way to get the shift.

Why not C as the base

C is the most memorable demo, but camera moves on every action get slow when a household adds ten items, and the shots need per-viewport tuning. Its best parts (light per phase, the in-world card, drag to a person) transplant cleanly onto A.

What would change this

If item positions must match the real apartment, pick B's drag-to-place regardless of base. If this is mainly a pitch or demo, C is worth its cost.
